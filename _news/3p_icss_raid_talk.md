On **20 October 2026**, I gave a [talk](https://www.kent.ac.uk/events/event/85020/small-free-and-effective-orchestrating-open-weight-small-language-models-to-outperform-single-llm-for-malware-analysis) at the [Institute of Cyber Security for Society (iCSS)](https://research.kent.ac.uk/cyber/) Seminar Series at the [University of Kent](https://www.kent.ac.uk/), on our RAID 2026 paper **[“Small, Free, and Effective: Orchestrating Open-Weight Small Language Models to Outperform Single LLM for Malware Analysis”](/assets/pdf/raid_2026.pdf)**. The seminar was held in a hybrid format, in the Kennedy Building and on MS Teams.

I shared how orchestrated ensembles of small, open-weight language models can match or exceed single LLMs on questions about malware detonation reports. Our best hybrid system, combining evidence-grounded pipelines with adversarial debate, outperformed both the strongest cyber-specialised model and the strongest ungrounded frontier LLM.

This work was done with my supervisors [Dr. Budi Arief](https://www.kent.ac.uk/school-of-computing/people/3056/arief-budi) and [Prof. Shujun Li](https://www.hooklee.com/).
